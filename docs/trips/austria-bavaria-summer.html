Munich, Bavaria & an alpine family resort

Shape: 10 nights. Five in Munich on foot and by train, then five at the Dachsteinkönig family resort in Gosau, Austria, with a car. The city has Raphael, Rembrandt, beer gardens, a monastery brewery and Neuschwanstein. The resort days are spent at the resort: pool, guided hikes, kids' club and an all-inclusive table nobody has to plan. The salt mine, the lakes and a falconry castle are there as options.

This matches the tracked trip "Austria & Bavaria" (MUC, 10 nights). It's written against plans/family-profile.md. Prices were checked in October 2026 from the sources listed at the end. Where sources disagreed, the higher figure is used. Anything not confirmed is marked check. Euros are converted at about $1.15 per €1.

Day by day

Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight. Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump 7 hours ahead, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's Flights section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

Day 1 — Arrive. English Garden. A gentle first afternoon. Watch the river surfers at the Eisbach wave, then go to the Chinesischer Turm beer garden. It has a playground and an old carousel, so the kids play while the parents have the first Maß of the trip.

Day 2 — Alte Pinakothek, then the Glockenspiel. The Raphael and Rembrandt morning. This is one of Europe's great old-master galleries: Raphael's Canigiani Holy Family and Tempi Madonna, Rembrandt's Passion series, Poussin, Rubens and Dürer. Under-18s are free. The gallery is partly being rearranged, so check those works are on view. Then go to Marienplatz for the Glockenspiel: the 11:00 show of jousting knights is a big hit with kids. Wander the Viktualienmarkt and have lunch at the Hofbräuhaus. It's loud, famous, and fine with kids at lunchtime.

Day 3 — The Residenz, then Hirschgarten. The Wittelsbach palace has the Treasury, with crowns, swords and jewels, which is the castle-lover's room. There's also the vast Renaissance Antiquarium. Under-18s are free but still need a reservation. In the afternoon, go to Hirschgarten, the biggest beer garden. It has a big climbing playground, a carousel, and a deer enclosure (a bonus, not the point).

Day 4 — Neuschwanstein by train. Take the regional train to Füssen (about 2 h), then the bus to Hohenschwangau. One Bayern-Ticket covers both parents, and the kids ride free (check the exact child rule on bahn.de). Walk or take the shuttle up to the Marienbrücke for the classic view, then tour Neuschwanstein in your timed slot. It's the longest day of the trip. The train time is the rest.

Day 5 — Andechs, the monastery brewery on the hill, then the Augustiner-Keller. Take the S8 to Herrsching (about 50 min) and walk up through the woods to Andechs, a Benedictine monastery that has brewed beer for centuries (about 45 min uphill; there's also a bus). Lunch in the beer garden under the chestnut trees. The playground sits right below the terrace, so parents can watch from their table. Walk back down to the Ammersee lakeshore at Herrsching before the train. Back in Munich, have dinner at the Augustiner-Keller, the city's oldest beer garden, which also has a playground.

Day 6 — Pick up the car. Salzburg's fortress on the way. Stop in Salzburg (1½ h) to ride the funicular up to Hohensalzburg, one of the largest intact medieval castles in Europe. Then drive on to Gosau (1 h).

Days 7–10 — At the resort. You're paying for an all-inclusive family resort, so these days are spent there. The car stays parked unless you pick something from Optional outings below. - Day 7: pool, and the outdoor area: climbing tower, trampolines, adventure playground and the kids' vehicle course. - Day 8: the resort's guided family hike. Ask at check-in what runs that week. - Day 9: kids' club for the children, and a spa morning for the parents. In the afternoon, everyone's back at the pool. - Day 10: a slow day before the flight.

Day 11 — Drive to MUC (2½–3 h), fly home. It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

Optional outings from the resort

Pick one or two if the resort days feel long, and leave the rest: - Hallstatt salt mine. Ride the new funicular up, then go underground in miners' clothes. The mine has wooden miners' slides, an underground salt lake, and a 7,000-year mining story. It reopens in August 2026 after a rebuild. Ages 4+ only. Afterwards, walk Hallstatt's old town. - Gosau lakes. The flat loop around Vorderer Gosausee (about an hour, stroller-friendly) has the Dachstein glacier reflected in the water. Add the Gosaukamm cable car up to the alpine pastures and summit playground. Ask the resort which lifts the guest card covers. - Hohenwerfen Castle (about 1 h). A cliff-top fortress with a falconry show: eagles and vultures flying over the valley.

Taken out

  • Nymphenburg Palace, as you asked. The Marstall coach museum was its kids' part.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

Bavaria is pork country, but the cheese-and-egg classics carry the vegetarian parent well: - Käsespätzle (egg noodles baked with mountain cheese), Obatzda (a spiced cheese spread with pretzels), Kaspressknödel (Austrian cheese dumplings, often in soup), and Eierschwammerl with dumplings in summer. A pretzel and Obatzda is a safe order at every beer garden. - Prinz Myshkin, near Marienplatz, has been Munich's vegetarian restaurant since 1984. The menu is international, with tofu and seitan for protein. - At the resort, the buffets are all-inclusive. Tell them about the vegetarian diet when booking; family resorts here usually cook for it.

Kids' backup: Schnitzel, Wiener sausages and pretzels are everywhere. Käsespätzle is basically mac and cheese.

Watch out for

  • German and Austrian school holidays. Bavaria's summer break begins at the start of August, and some Austrian states start in early July. A June or early-July trip avoids the busiest weeks at both the castle and the resort.
  • Austria needs a motorway vignette (a digital sticker) on the rental car. Check that it's included if you rent in Germany.
  • Car seats are required in Germany and Austria for children under 150 cm.
  • Neuschwanstein can only be seen inside on a timed guided tour. Kids' tickets are free, but each one costs the €2.50 fee and must be booked.
