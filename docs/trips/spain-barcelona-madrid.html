Spain — Barcelona & Madrid

Shape: 10 nights. Five in Barcelona: Gaudí, a castle on a hill, a rack railway to a mountain monastery, and the beach. Then the fast train (AVE) to Madrid for five: the Prado, the Royal Palace's armoury, and a fairy-tale castle in Segovia. It's walking, metro and trains the whole way; no car.

Day by day

Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight.Fly STL → FRA → BCN · 13h 25m · Fri Jun 4 3:30p – Sat Jun 5 11:55a Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump 7 hours ahead, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's Flights section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

Day 1 — Arrive. The beach.Taxi Barcelona airport (BCN) → Apartment, Eixample · 30m · Sat Jun 5 12:55p – 1:25p (an hour after landing) · a van taxi for six Settle in, then spend the late afternoon at Barceloneta with paddling and sandcastles. Spain eats late, so an early tapas dinner is fine.

Day 2 — Sagrada Família, then Park Güell.Sun Jun 6: Leave 8:30a (metro L5) · arrive 8:45a · Sagrada Família, first slot (9:00) Take the first slot at the Sagrada Família. Children under 11 are free, so only the parents pay. Inside, the stained glass turns the stone forest blue and orange. In the afternoon, Park Güell: the mosaic dragon, the gingerbread houses and the long wavy bench (timed tickets).

Day 3 — Montjuïc: cable car and castle. Ride the cable car up to Montjuïc Castle, a star-shaped fortress with cannons and harbour views. On the way down, the Olympic stadium and the gardens. Back in town, the Magic Fountain shows run on summer evenings (check; they were paused during the 2024 drought).

Day 4 — Gothic Quarter and La Boqueria. Wander the Gothic Quarter's lanes to the cathedral. Its cloister keeps 13 white geese, a centuries-old tradition (a bonus). Then go to La Boqueria market for fruit cups and a counter lunch. Spend the afternoon at the beach or the pool.

Day 5 — Montserrat by rack railway.Wed Jun 9: Leave 8:15a (R5 train from Plaça Espanya (~1 h), then the rack railway) · arrive 10:15a · Montserrat monastery; the boys' choir sings about 1:00p (check) Take the train an hour out, then the rack railway (cremallera) up the serrated mountain to the monastery. The boys' choir, one of Europe's oldest, sings in the basilica around 1 pm on most days (check the schedule). Funiculars climb higher for short, easy mountain walks. It's the trains day of the trip.

Day 6 — AVE to MadridTrain Barcelona Sants → Madrid Atocha · 2h 30m · Thu Jun 10 10:00a – 12:30p · AVE; metro or taxi to Sants ~20 min, so leave by 9:25a (about 2½–3 h). In the afternoon, go to Retiro Park: the Crystal Palace, playgrounds, and puppet shows on weekends.

Day 7 — The Prado.Fri Jun 11: Leave 9:30a (a 20-minute walk) · arrive 9:50a · Prado opens 10:00 Arrive at opening and stay about 2½ hours. Under-18s are free. See Raphael's Holy Family ("La Perla") and Portrait of a Cardinal, Velázquez's Las Meninas (ask the kids to find the painter in the picture), Caravaggio's David with the Head of Goliath, Rubens, Goya, and Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights, which kids can study for ages. Have chocolate con churros at San Ginés after.

Day 8 — The Royal Palace and its armoury.Sat Jun 12: Leave 9:35a (a 15-minute walk) · arrive 9:50a · Royal Palace opens 10:00 Europe's largest royal palace by floor area. The Royal Armoury has suits of armour for kings, children and horses, and is the castle-lover's room of Madrid. Then the Plaza Mayor and the Mercado de San Miguel for lunch.

Day 9 — Segovia.Sun Jun 13: Leave 8:20a (metro to Chamartín, the ~9:00a AVE (27 min), then the bus into town) · arrive 10:00a · Segovia's Alcázar opens 10:00 Take the AVE (27 min). Stand under the 2,000-year-old Roman aqueduct, then go to the Alcázar, the turreted castle on a cliff that looks straight out of a storybook. Climb the tower for the view.

Day 10 — Madrid, slow. Choose: the Thyssen (Caravaggio, Holbein's Henry VIII, Ghirlandaio's Giovanna Tornabuoni), or a lazy pool and park day.

Day 11 — Fly homeTaxi Apartment near Sol → Madrid airport (MAD) · 35m · Tue Jun 15Fly MAD → STL · Tue Jun 15 · times appear after the next flight check from MAD. It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

Options

  • Toledo instead of Segovia (AVE, 33 min). A walled hilltop city, with El Greco and a sword-making tradition.
  • Tibidabo's funicular for the view over Barcelona. The amusement park at the top is skipped, since theme parks are out.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

  • Tortilla española (potato and egg omelette) is everywhere, and the kids eat it too. Add garbanzos con espinacas (chickpeas with spinach), pimientos de padrón, Manchego cheese and pan con tomate.
  • Barcelona: Teresa Carles is a well-known vegetarian restaurant.
  • Others: seafood paella, grilled fish, jamón.
  • Kids' backup: croquetas, tortilla, pasta, and churros as the reward.

Beer: Barcelona's Fàbrica Moritz, the city's historic brewery, now a bar. Madrid has a growing craft-beer scene in Malasaña.

Watch out for

  • Dinner is at 9 pm in Spain. Plan a big late lunch and an early tapas supper, which suits the kids better.
  • Heat: Madrid in July is 35°C+. Do the outdoor sights before noon, and keep the pool or a museum for the afternoon.
  • Pickpockets on the Barcelona metro and Las Ramblas.
