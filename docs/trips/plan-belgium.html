Shape: 9 nights. This is the Trappist beer trip the profile was waiting for, and it's built on trains, where children under 12 ride free. It has two castles, the Ghent Altarpiece, Michelangelo's only sculpture to leave Italy in his lifetime, Rubens in Antwerp, a chocolate workshop, a tram ride into a cave, and a café with a playground next to the Westvleteren abbey.

Fly into and out of Brussels (BRU). The airport has its own train station, and Bruges is about 1½ h away.

Day by day

Day 1 — Arrive. Train to Bruges. Walk the canals and the Markt, and have frites on a bench.

Day 2 — Bruges: belfry, Michelangelo, chocolate. Climb the Belfry (366 steps, so maybe a parent stays down with the youngest; check the age rules). Then see Michelangelo's Madonna of Bruges in the Church of Our Lady, the only sculpture of his to leave Italy in his lifetime. In the afternoon, take a chocolate-making workshop, the local making-of. Finish with a short canal boat ride (30 min).

Day 3 — Ghent (25 min by train). Gravensteen is a real 12th-century moated castle with ramparts and a torture museum the 8-year-old will love. Under-13s are free. Then the Ghent Altarpiece (van Eyck's Adoration of the Mystic Lamb) in St Bavo's Cathedral, and dinner on the Graslei.

Day 4 — Westvleteren and Ypres (rental car). Lunch at In de Vrede, the café opposite St Sixtus Abbey. It's the only place you can drink Westvleteren at the source, and it has an outdoor play area. Then go to Ypres (20 min): the In Flanders Fields Museum in the rebuilt Cloth Hall, and the Menin Gate. The Last Post is sounded every evening at 8 pm, which is late but moving.

Day 5 — Train to Brussels. See the Grand Place, Manneken Pis and a waffle. In the afternoon, the Belgian Comic Strip Center: Tintin and the Smurfs in an Art Nouveau building.

Day 6 — The Old Masters, then the Atomium. The Royal Museums of Fine Arts have Bruegel, Rubens, Jordaens and the Flemish primitives. Afterwards, go up the Atomium, the giant steel molecule. You ride an escalator through the tubes.

Day 7 — Antwerp (40 min). The Cathedral of Our Lady has two Rubens altarpieces, The Raising of the Cross and The Descent from the Cross. Then Het Steen, the riverside castle. Antwerp Central station is one of the world's most beautiful, which earns trains points. The Rubens House is closed for restoration (only the garden is open). Parents' option: Café Trappisten opposite Westmalle Abbey, a bus ride from Antwerp.

Day 8 — The Han caves. Take the train to the Ardennes, then a short bus. At the Grottes de Han, a vintage tram takes you into the forest to enormous caves with an underground river. Nearby is Rochefort, home of the Trappist beer; the abbey itself is closed to visitors.

Day 9 — Brussels, slow. Choose: Cantillon, a working lambic brewery and museum (a making-of, check tours), or parks and shopping.

Day 10 — Fly home from BRU.

The Trappist map

Of the world's handful of Trappist breweries, five of Belgium's six can be reached on this trip: - Westvleteren: In de Vrede (Day 4), with a playground. - Westmalle: Café Trappisten (option on Day 7). - Rochefort: in the town (Day 8); the abbey isn't open. - Chimay: the abbey's inn, the Auberge de Poteaupré. It's too far for this route; consider it for a car-based variant. - Orval: you can visit the abbey ruins. Same note as Chimay.

Every bar in Belgium pours the rest.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

  • Kaaskroketten (cheese croquettes), stoemp (mash with vegetables, often with an egg), Flemish asparagus with egg (spring), and a great deal of cheese, including Trappist abbey cheeses.
  • Others: moules-frites (mussels and fries) and Flemish beef stew cooked in beer.
  • Kids' backup: frites, waffles, and spaghetti bolognese, which is on every Belgian café menu.

Watch out for

  • In de Vrede is closed on Fridays. Check its calendar before fixing the Westvleteren day.
  • Belgian weather: bring rain layers even in July.
  • Cobblestones: bring a stroller with real wheels for the 3-year-old.
