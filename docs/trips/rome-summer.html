Rome

Day by day

Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight. Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump 7 hours ahead, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's Flights section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

Day 1 — Arrive. An evening walk: Piazza Navona's Bernini fountain, then the Pantheon, and gelato at Giolitti.

Day 2 — The Vatican.Sun Jul 11: Leave 7:30a (taxi or a 25-minute walk) · arrive 7:45a · Vatican Museums, first slot (about 8:00) Take the earliest slot. The Raphael Rooms are the reason to come: The School of Athens, The Fire in the Borgo, The Liberation of St Peter. Add the Sistine Chapel (check that the altar wall restoration has finished), the Laocoön and the map gallery. In the afternoon, see St Peter's: Michelangelo's Pietà and Bernini's canopy. Then climb the dome; the kids will remember the narrow leaning stair.

Day 3 — The Colosseum and the Forum.Mon Jul 12: Leave 7:55a (taxi or bus) · arrive 8:20a · Colosseum, first slot (about 8:30) Arrive at opening, before the heat. Walk the Forum and the Palatine Hill, where the emperors lived. Rest in the afternoon. In the evening, see the Trevi Fountain after dark and the Spanish Steps.

Day 4 — Galleria Borghese.Tue Jul 13: Leave 8:05a (taxi) · arrive 8:30a · Galleria Borghese, 9:00 slot (collect tickets 30 min ahead) It's the profile's perfect museum: small and first-rate. Bernini's Apollo and Daphne and Rape of Proserpina (the hand in the marble thigh), Caravaggio's Boy with a Basket of Fruit, and Raphael's Deposition. Visits are timed 2-hour slots. Afterwards, rent a family bike or a surrey in the Villa Borghese park, and catch the sunset at the Pincio terrace.

Day 5 — Castel Sant'Angelo, then Caravaggio's chapels. Hadrian's tomb became the popes' fortress. It has ramparts, cannonballs, a secret escape corridor to the Vatican, and the angel on top. In the afternoon, see three Caravaggios for free in San Luigi dei Francesi (the St Matthew cycle), then the Galleria Doria Pamphilj, with Velázquez's Innocent X and more Caravaggio. Have dinner in Trastevere.

Day 6 — Ostia Antica.Thu Jul 15: Leave 8:30a (metro to Piramide, then the Roma–Lido train (~30 min)) · arrive 9:30a · Ostia Antica opens 8:30 Take the train 30 min to Rome's ancient port: a whole Roman town of streets, shops, a theatre and latrines, with room to roam and few crowds. It's a better kids' ruin than the Forum. Go to the beach at Ostia after, if everyone still has energy.

Day 7 — Galleria Colonna or a pasta class. The Galleria Colonna, a favourite from the profile, opens only on certain days, usually Friday and Saturday mornings (check). Otherwise, take a family pasta class, or the gladiator school near the Appian Way, where the kids train with wooden swords (check ages).

Day 8 — Fly home from FCO. It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

Gelato

The well-known names: Giolitti (near the Pantheon, since 1900), Fatamorgana (unusual flavours, several branches), Gelateria del Teatro (near Navona), and Otaleg in Trastevere.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

  • Roman classics with legumes: pasta e ceci (chickpeas) and pasta e fagioli (beans).
  • Cheese dishes: cacio e pepe, supplì (fried rice balls with mozzarella), and Roman-Jewish carciofi (artichokes) in the Ghetto.
  • Vegetarian restaurants: Il Margutta, vegetarian since 1979.
  • Pizza al taglio by weight at Bonci's Pizzarium near the Vatican.
  • Kids' backup: pasta in bianco, pizza, supplì.

Watch out for

  • Heat: Rome in July is 33°C+. Do the outdoor ruins at opening.
  • Dress code at St Peter's and the Vatican: covered shoulders and knees for everyone.
  • Fake "skip the line" sellers outside the Vatican. Buy only on the official sites.
