Greece — Athens & Naxos

Shape: 10 nights. Four in Athens for the Acropolis, the guards in pom-pom shoes, and the museum with the 2,000-year-old computer. Then six on Naxos, the family island: shallow sandy beaches, a Venetian castle quarter, a giant marble statue lying in a field, and a distillery that makes the island's citron liqueur.

Day by day

Day 1 — Arrive. Plaka's lanes, and dinner with an Acropolis view.

Day 2 — The Acropolis, at 8 am. Go at opening, before the heat and the cruise groups. Then the Acropolis Museum, which needs a separate ticket. It has the original Caryatids and the Parthenon frieze at eye level: great sculpture by the profile's own definition. Rest at midday.

Day 3 — The Agora and the guards. The Ancient Agora has the Temple of Hephaestus, the best-preserved Greek temple anywhere, with room to roam. Watch the changing of the Evzones guard at Syntagma (on the hour; the big parade is Sunday at 11). The National Garden has a playground. At sunset, take the Lycabettus funicular up for the view.

Day 4 — The National Archaeological Museum. It has the Antikythera mechanism, a bronze gear-driven "computer" from 2,000 years ago (it's unique, so it passes the profile's test). There's also the gold Mask of Agamemnon and the great bronze Zeus/Poseidon and the Jockey of Artemision. Or see Cape Sounion's temple at sunset (1½ h by car or bus).

Day 5 — To Naxos. Take the morning fast ferry or a flight. In the evening, watch the sunset at the Portara, the giant marble doorway of an unfinished temple, on a causeway by the harbour.

Day 6 — Beach: Agios Prokopios or Plaka beach. These are long sandy beaches with shallow, clear water, ideal for the 3- and 5-year-olds. Have dinner in the Kastro, the Venetian castle quarter on top of the old town.

Day 7 — The mountain villages. In Halki, the Vallindras kitron distillery shows how the island's citron liqueur is made (a local making-of). See the marble-paved village of Apeiranthos. On the way back, stop at the Kouros of Melanes, a 6-metre marble statue left unfinished in a quarry 2,600 years ago, lying in an orchard.

Day 8 — Beach day.

Day 9 — The Temple of Demeter and a farm lunch. Visit the restored temple in the island's centre. Have lunch at a taverna with Naxos potatoes and graviera cheese. Spend the afternoon on the beach.

Day 10 — Beach, slow. Or take a short boat to Koufonisia's turquoise coves, if the crew feels up to a boat day.

Day 11 — Fly or ferry to Athens, and home. Same-day connections from Naxos are tight, so consider one night near the Athens airport (Day 10 evening). It's a cheap insurance policy.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

Greece is the easiest country on the list for this: - Gigantes (giant baked beans), fava (yellow split-pea purée), revithia (chickpea stew), and halloumi or saganaki (fried cheese). - Also spanakopita, horiatiki salad with feta, and Greek yogurt with honey every morning. - Grilled fish and octopus for the others. - Kids' backup: souvlaki pita, pasta, and loukoumades (honey doughnuts).

Watch out for

  • Heat: Athens in July is 35–40°C. Ruins at opening only; afternoons in the shade or the pool.
  • The meltemi wind in July–August can make the fast ferries rough. That's one more reason to price the flight.
  • Island car rental: confirm a 7-seater and car seats in advance.
