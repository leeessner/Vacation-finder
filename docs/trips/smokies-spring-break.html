Great Smoky Mountains

Shape: 4 nights in a 3-bedroom cabin near Gatlinburg, by car (about 8 h each way, by choice). The park itself is free. You get a valley of old log cabins and a working mill, waterfalls the kids can walk to, the longest pedestrian suspension bridge in North America, and a steam-era railroad on the far side of the mountains.

Before you go: mid-March in the park

  • Kuwohi (formerly Clingmans Dome) road is usually closed until about April 1, so the highest point is out for a mid-March trip.
  • Laurel Falls, the easy paved waterfall walk, was closed for major repairs. Check that it has reopened.
  • Weather swings: snow is possible at the top of Newfound Gap Road, while Gatlinburg is in the 60s°F. The road closes when it ices.

Day by day

Day 1 (Fri) — Drive (about 8 h: STL → Nashville → Knoxville). Stop for lunch in Nashville at about the halfway point. Arrive, light the fire, and make dinner in the cabin.

Day 2 (Sat) — Cades Cove. An 11-mile one-way loop through a mountain valley. See the log cabins, churches and the Cable Mill, a working water-powered gristmill (a making-of). Deer and wild turkey are common; black bears are possible (a bonus, from the car). Go early; Saturdays are busy. Pack a picnic. On the way back, walk the short trail to Meigs Falls' viewpoint or the riverside paths at The Sinks.

Day 3 (Sun) — Gatlinburg: SkyBridge, then Elkmont. Take the SkyLift chairlift up to the SkyBridge. It's the longest pedestrian suspension bridge in North America, with a glass panel in the middle. In the afternoon, go to Elkmont, an abandoned 1920s resort village whose restored cabins the kids can explore. Then walk the flat Little River Trail along the river.

Day 4 (Mon) — Over the mountains to the railroad. Drive Newfound Gap Road over the crest of the Smokies (if it's open), with a stop at the state line. On the North Carolina side, go to the Oconaluftee Mountain Farm Museum and Mingus Mill, another working mill. Continue to Bryson City for the Great Smoky Mountains Railroad, a vintage train excursion along the river (check that the spring season has started by mid-March). It's the trains day.

Day 5 (Tue) — Drive home.

Options

  • Ober Mountain aerial tramway from downtown Gatlinburg: a big cable car up to the mountain. It's an easier "ride" than the SkyLift.
  • Grotto Falls (walk behind a waterfall) is on Roaring Fork Motor Nature Trail, which usually opens later in spring (check).
  • Dollywood is skipped: theme parks are out.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

  • The cabin kitchen does the heavy lifting: eggs, beans, cheese, and veggie chili.
  • Out: Gatlinburg's pancake houses (the Pancake Pantry is the original), and BBQ for the others.
  • Kids' backup: pancakes, mac and cheese, burgers.

Beer: Smoky Mountain Brewery in Gatlinburg is a family restaurant with its own beer. Moonshine tastings for the parents, if curious.

Watch out for

  • The 8-hour drive for 4 nights is the trip's cost. Leave by 7 am on Friday.
  • Pigeon Forge traffic on the Parkway is notorious. Use the back roads between the cabin and the park.
  • Bears: never leave food in the car or on the cabin deck.
