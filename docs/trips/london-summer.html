London & York

Shape: 9 nights. Six in London, with the two Tudor and royal palaces as day trips. Then three in York, two hours north by train, for the National Railway Museum, Vikings and medieval walls. Everything is by foot, Tube and train. No car.

This builds on the "England with Kids" post (London 4 → Bath 3 → York 3), adds Hampton Court as requested, and gives London more room because so much of the art is there. Bath is the alternative to York; see York or Bath?

Written against plans/family-profile.md. Prices were checked in October 2026 from the sources listed at the end. Anything not confirmed is marked check. Pounds are converted at about $1.30 per £1.

Why South Kensington, rather than the tracker's provisional Trafalgar Square: the V&A, with its Raphael Cartoons, is around the corner. So is Kensington Gardens, with the Diana Memorial Playground, whose pirate ship is the best playground in London. The Tube runs direct to Heathrow.

Fly into Heathrow (LHR). Fly home from LHR, or from Manchester (MAN), 1½ h from York, if fares allow. The tracker can price both.

Day by day

Day 1 — Arrive. Kensington Gardens. The Diana Memorial Playground's pirate ship, then an early night.

Day 2 — Westminster and the National Gallery. Big Ben and Parliament, then Changing of the Guard at Buckingham Palace (about 11:00 on set days; check the schedule). Picnic in St James's Park. In the afternoon, go to the National Gallery, which is free. The art is world-class: Raphael, Leonardo's Virgin of the Rocks, Caravaggio's Supper at Emmaus, Rembrandt's late self-portrait and Poussin. Keep it to an hour of the highlights, then let the kids climb on the Trafalgar Square lions.

Day 3 — Tower of London. The Crown Jewels, a Yeoman Warder ("Beefeater") tour, the armour in the White Tower and the ravens. The profile names it specifically. Walk across Tower Bridge to Borough Market for lunch.

Day 4 — Hampton Court Palace. Henry VIII's palace is 35 minutes by train from Waterloo. It has the Tudor kitchens, the maze (included in the ticket) and the Magic Garden play area with its dragon (check whether it's included or ticketed on your date). The gardens run down to the Thames.

Day 5 — The V&A, then dinosaurs. The Raphael Cartoons at the V&A are seven huge designs for the Sistine Chapel tapestries. It's the best Raphael room outside Italy, and it's free. Next door are the Cast Courts, with a full-size cast of Michelangelo's David and Trajan's Column. Spend the afternoon at the Natural History Museum (free; book a slot) for the dinosaurs and the blue whale. It's from the England post, so keep it short.

Day 6 — Windsor Castle. The oldest occupied castle in the world is 40 minutes by train, which makes it a castle day for the kids. Alternative, staying in town: the Wallace Collection (free). It holds a superb armoury, Rembrandt, Poussin and Titian in a mansion, and it's one of the "small museum, first-rate work" places the profile describes.

Day 7 — Train to York (2 h from King's Cross). In the afternoon, walk a stretch of the City Walls, which are free and the most complete in England.

Day 8 — National Railway Museum. It's free and the trains day of the trip. Mallard, the world's fastest steam locomotive, a Japanese bullet train, the royal carriages and a miniature railway (ticketed). Then the Shambles, York's medieval shopping lane, for an old-town wander.

Day 9 — York Minster and the Vikings. York Minster is one of Europe's great Gothic cathedrals. JORVIK Viking Centre has a ride through a rebuilt Viking street, with sights, sounds and smells. Book a time slot. Climb Clifford's Tower, the castle keep, for the view.

Day 10 — Home. Take a morning train to London and the Elizabeth line to Heathrow, for an afternoon flight. Or fly from Manchester.

York or Bath?

York Bath
For the kids Trains (NRM), a Viking ride, walls to walk, a castle keep The Roman Baths (good kids' audio guide), Royal Crescent lawns
For the parents The Minster, medieval streets Georgian architecture, the Abbey
Logistics 2 h direct from King's Cross 1½ h from Paddington

Recommendation: York. It hits three of the kids' four loves (trains, castles, old towns), while Bath's main draw is Roman. Bath's thermal spa doesn't admit under-16s.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

London is the easiest city on the list for this: - Indian food is the best protein bet: dal, paneer, chana (chickpeas). Dishoom is a family favourite and books ahead. Its black dal is famous. - Mildreds is long-running and vegetarian. - Pubs routinely have a vegetarian main and fish and chips for the others. - In York, try Indian restaurants and the cafés in the Shambles area.

Kids' backup: burgers, as the profile says. Honest Burgers is a reliable chain. Fish and chips is the obvious local food.

Beer: London's microbrewery scene (the Bermondsey "Beer Mile" taprooms, Saturdays) is for the parents. Kid-friendly historic pubs work at lunchtime. For Belgian Trappists, a Belgium trip would beat England.

Watch out for

  • UK school holidays start in late July, so June and early July are quieter.
  • Changing of the Guard doesn't happen every day. Check the schedule for your dates.
  • Big museums are free but busy. Book the free time slots where offered (Natural History Museum).
