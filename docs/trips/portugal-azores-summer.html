Portugal — Porto, Lisbon & the Algarve

Shape: 10 nights. Three in Porto for port wine, the bridges and a vintage tram. Four in Lisbon for its castle, Belém and Sintra's palaces. Three in Lagos on the Algarve for the cliffs and the golden beaches. This is the Porto → Lisbon → Lagos route from the Instagram carousel in plans/inspiration.md.

This replaces the tracker's current "Portugal & the Azores" idea, which is kept as an option below. Written against plans/family-profile.md. Prices were checked in October 2026; anything not confirmed is marked check. Euros are converted at about $1.15 per €1.

Fly into Porto (OPO) and home from Lisbon (LIS). The car is picked up in Lisbon on Day 8 and dropped at the airport on Day 11.

Day by day

Day 1 — Arrive in Porto. Walk down to the Ribeira riverfront and have dinner by the Douro.

Day 2 — Port wine. Cross the Dom Luís I bridge on its upper deck to Vila Nova de Gaia, where every port house has its cellars. Tour one: Graham's and Taylor's are the classics. It's the profile's own example of a "local making-of" experience. Kids come along and get grape juice; parents taste. Ride the Gaia cable car back down to the river.

Day 3 — Trams and tiles. See the blue-tile walls of São Bento station, then go to Livraria Lello, the Harry Potter-ish bookshop (timed tickets). In the afternoon, take the vintage tram line 1 along the river to Foz, where the Douro meets the Atlantic, for the beach and the lighthouse.

Day 4 — Train to Lisbon (Alfa Pendular, about 3 h). In the evening, ride tram 28 through the Alfama, or take an easier stroll in the Baixa.

Day 5 — Belém. See the Belém Tower (a little fort in the river) and the Jerónimos Monastery. The National Coach Museum's golden royal carriages are the kids' part. Have pastéis de Belém where the custard tart was invented.

Day 6 — Sintra. Take the train from Rossio (40 min). Pena Palace is a red-and-yellow hilltop castle out of a fairy tale. The Moorish Castle next door has battlements to walk along a ridge. Alternatively, go to Quinta da Regaleira: its Initiation Well spirals down into tunnels the kids will run through. Pick two of the three; the hills are steep.

Day 7 — São Jorge Castle and the old masters. In the morning, São Jorge Castle has ramparts, towers and the best view of Lisbon. In the afternoon, the National Museum of Ancient Art has Bosch's Temptation of St Anthony, Dürer, Holbein and a Raphael panel (check what's on view). It also has the Japanese screens showing the Portuguese arriving in Japan.

Day 8 — Drive to the Algarve (about 3 h). The beach that afternoon.

Day 9 — Ponta da Piedade and the beaches. Walk the cliff-top boardwalk at Ponta da Piedade: sea arches, grottoes and golden rock. Go down the steps to Praia do Camilo or Praia Dona Ana, which are among the most photographed beaches in Europe. These are the profile's "beaches that are different from home".

Day 10 — Sagres. Drive 40 min to the Fortress of Sagres on the cliffs at the edge of Europe, then swim at Praia da Mareta. Or keep it a pool day.

Day 11 — Drive to LIS (about 3 h) and fly home.

Options

  • Benagil Cave is famous, but it's reached only by boat or kayak, and the family isn't big on boats. Skip it, or take a short tour from Lagos.
  • The Azores instead of the Algarve (the tracker's current idea). Fly Lisbon → Ponta Delgada and stay 4–5 nights on São Miguel for crater lakes, hot springs and tea plantations. It's wilder and greener, but it's another flight for six and needs a car.
  • The Gulbenkian Museum in Lisbon has Rembrandt, Rubens and a superb small collection. It's a calm half-day.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

Portugal is fish-first, which is great for everyone else. For the vegetarian parent: - Chickpea salads (salada de grão), feijoada vegetariana (bean stew), eggs (ovos), and Queijo da Serra cheese. - Ask for caldo verde "sem chouriço" (kale-and-potato soup without the sausage). - Say "sem carne" (without meat); vegetarian is common in Lisbon and Porto now. - Others: grilled sardines, bacalhau, piri-piri chicken. - Kids' backup: piri-piri chicken, pasta, and pastéis de nata daily.

Port wine is the making-of. Beer: the Super Bock Casa da Cerveja near Porto offers brewery tours.

Watch out for

  • Sintra's hills and crowds. Use the shuttle bus between palaces, not your feet, and arrive at opening.
  • Algarve sea is Atlantic: cold and with waves. Pick sheltered coves for the youngest.
  • Tolls on Algarve motorways are electronic. Get the rental's transponder.
