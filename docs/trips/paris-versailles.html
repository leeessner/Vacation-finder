Paris, Versailles & Alsace

Shape: 9 nights. Six in Paris, covering the family's own list: the Louvre, the Orsay, Montmartre, the Eiffel Tower, a park, Versailles and one Disneyland day. Then three in Strasbourg, 1¾ hours east by TGV, with Colmar and a mountain-top castle as day trips. It's trains the whole way, with no car. The last train runs straight to Paris-CDG for the flight home.

This matches the tracked trip "Paris & Versailles" (9 nights). It adds the Alsace idea from the Colmar-with-kids post, which is in plans/inspiration.md. Written against plans/family-profile.md. Prices were checked in October 2026 from the sources listed at the end. Anything not confirmed is marked check. Euros are converted at about $1.15 per €1.

Why the Luxembourg Gardens, rather than the tracker's current center at the Hôtel de Ville: the gardens are Paris's best park for children, with a big playground, toy sailboats on the pond and a puppet theatre. It's the profile's "a park" built into every day. The Louvre, Orsay and Notre-Dame are a short walk or Métro ride away.

Fly into Paris-CDG. Fly home from CDG; the TGV runs from Strasbourg directly to the airport station.

Day by day

Day 1 — Arrive. The Luxembourg Gardens. Rent a wooden toy sailboat to push around the Grand Bassin, then go to the playground. An easy first afternoon.

Day 2 — The Louvre, first thing. Take the 9:00 slot and plan about 2½ hours. Raphael's Belle Jardinière and Baldassare Castiglione; Leonardo's Mona Lisa, Virgin of the Rocks and Virgin and Child with St Anne; Michelangelo's two Slaves; the Winged Victory; Caravaggio, Poussin's great room, and Rembrandt. Picnic in the Tuileries, which has trampolines for the kids.

Day 3 — Sainte-Chapelle, Notre-Dame, the Eiffel Tower. Go early to Sainte-Chapelle, a room made of stained glass. Then Notre-Dame, which reopened in December 2024 and is free to enter. In the afternoon, take the lift to the Eiffel Tower's summit; book the slot ahead. Afterwards, the Champ de Mars has playgrounds and lawns. If you can stay out, the tower sparkles for five minutes on the hour after dark.

Day 4 — Versailles. Take the RER C, about 40 minutes. Tour the palace early. Under-18s are free but need a timed slot. Then get out into the park: rent bikes or a rowing boat on the Grand Canal, or take the little train to the Trianons and Marie Antoinette's Hamlet, a storybook farm village. On summer weekends the fountains run to music.

Day 5 — The Orsay in the morning, Montmartre in the evening. Start at the Orsay at opening. Impressionism is "nice, not essential" in the profile, but the sculpture hall (Carpeaux, Rodin) and the giant station clock work for everyone. Keep it to about 1½ hours. Rest in the afternoon. In the evening, ride the funicular up to Sacré-Cœur, watch the painters in Place du Tertre, and have dinner in the village lanes. Then sit on the Sacré-Cœur steps as the city lights come on.

Day 6 — Disneyland Paris. The one special day, as the profile allows. It's 40 minutes on the RER A. Rope-drop at opening, and plan around the 3-year-old's nap.

Day 7 — TGV to Strasbourg (1¾ h). In the afternoon, go to Petite France, the half-timbered, canal-side old quarter. Then climb the Barrage Vauban, a 17th-century covered bridge with a free roof terrace over the canals.

Day 8 — The cathedral and its astronomical clock. At 12:30 each day, the clock's mechanical apostles parade and the rooster crows. It's a big hit with kids. Buy the ticket that morning; check the times. Climb the platform for the view if legs allow. The afternoon is for Parc de l'Orangerie: playground, rowing boats and the storks (a bonus).

Day 9 — The castle, then Colmar. Take the train to Sélestat (25 min), then the shuttle bus (line 500, which runs in the tourist season; check the times) up to Haut-Koenigsbourg. It's a huge red sandstone castle on a mountain top, with ramparts, an armoury and views over the Rhine plain. It's the castle day of the trip. In the afternoon, take the train to Colmar (12 min): Little Venice, a short flat-bottomed boat ride on the canal (25 min), and old-town lanes. Parents can add the Isenheim Altarpiece at the Unterlinden museum (children under 12 free).

Day 10 — TGV to Paris-CDG (about 2 h), then fly home.

Optional

  • Musée Rodin. It's the profile's "great sculpture" stop: The Thinker and The Gates of Hell stand in a garden where the kids can roam while the parents look. It fits a free afternoon, such as Day 5's rest time.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

  • Paris: falafel in the Marais (L'As du Fallafel is the famous one), which gives chickpea protein and is a kids' favourite. Buckwheat galettes with egg and cheese, served in any crêperie. Creperies are also the kids' backup, along with steak-frites and pasta.
  • Alsace: tarte flambée (flammekueche) has a vegetarian version without bacon, and every winstub makes it. Bibeleskaas, herbed fresh cheese with potatoes, is protein-rich. There's also Munster cheese, and spätzle.
  • Vegetarian is normal in Paris now. Most bistros have a vegetarian main; ask for sans viande.

Beer: Alsace is France's beer country. Look for Strasbourg's microbreweries and brasseries, plus Meteor, a family brewery north of the city. Paris has good craft-beer bars for the parents.

Watch out for

  • French school holidays start in early July. June is calmer, especially at Versailles.
  • Museum closing days: the Louvre is closed Tuesdays, Versailles and the Orsay are closed Mondays. Arrange Days 2–5 around them when the dates are fixed.
  • Pickpockets on the Métro and around the Eiffel Tower: keep phones and wallets zipped.
