Florence & Tuscany

Shape: 8 nights. Five on the farm above Florence, three in the countryside near Siena with a car. Florence is the art; the countryside is the castle, the towers, a vineyard and the cheese. The days are fuller than the first draft, as you asked. Nothing was dropped: the extra choices sit under Options for spare time or swaps.

Written against plans/family-profile.md. Prices were checked in October 2026 from the sources listed at the end. Where those sources disagreed, the higher figure is used, and anything not confirmed is marked check. Euros are converted at about $1.15 per €1.

Day by day

One big thing in the morning and another in the afternoon. The farm's pool is there for the evenings. Gelato every day: the best scoops are listed under Gelato, and each day names the one on the way.

Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight. Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump 7 hours ahead, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's Flights section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

Day 1 — Arrive. Settle in at Fattoria di Maiano: pool and the farm. Dinner at Cucina Pietraforte, within walking distance (from the Instagram post; check it's open).

Day 2 — Bargello, Pitti & Boboli, then sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo.Sun Jun 6: Leave 7:35a (bus 7 or a taxi into town, then a short walk) · arrive 8:05a · Bargello opens 8:15Sun Jun 6: Leave 7:45p (taxi from the farm) · arrive 8:15p · Sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo (about 8:50p in June) Start at the Bargello at 8:15, the great sculpture museum: Donatello's David and Michelangelo's Bacchus. It's small, so plan about an hour. Walk across the Ponte Vecchio to the Pitti Palace. Its Palatine Gallery is the Raphael room of the trip, with the Madonna della Seggiola and La Velata. Then let the kids loose in the Boboli Gardens: grottoes, statues and big lawns. Go back to the farm for a pool break and an early dinner. Come back down for sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo, the whole city laid out below. In June the sun sets around 8:50 pm, so it's a late night; the farm is 20 minutes away. Gelato: La Carraia, by the bridge on the Pitti side.

Day 3 — Michelangelo's David, then Leonardo's machines.Mon Jun 7: Leave 7:35a (bus 7 to San Marco, then a 5-minute walk) · arrive 8:05a · Accademia, 8:15 slot Take the first slot at the Accademia (8:15) and allow about an hour. The combined Accademia + Bargello ticket is valid for 48 hours, so it covers Days 2 and 3. Walk past the Duomo and the Baptistery's bronze doors. In the afternoon, the Leonardo Interactive Museum has 50+ working models of his inventions to crank and push. It's the one science museum on the trip, because it's unique. Gelato: Carabè, near the Accademia, for Sicilian granita too.

Day 4 — Uffizi, then a pasta class.Tue Jun 8: Leave 7:30a (bus 7 or a taxi) · arrive 8:00a · Uffizi, 8:15 slot Book the 8:15 slot and keep the visit to about 2 hours. Go straight for Raphael's Madonna of the Goldfinch, Leonardo's Annunciation, Michelangelo's Doni Tondo and Caravaggio's Medusa. In the afternoon, take a family pasta class: tagliatelle or ravioli, with a "little chef" diploma for the kids at some schools. Dinner is what you made. Gelato: Vivoli, near Santa Croce, the city's oldest.

Day 5 — Day trip by train: Parmesan + Bologna.Wed Jun 9: Leave 6:30a (taxi to Santa Maria Novella, the 7:05a fast train to Bologna, then a train or taxi to the dairy) · arrive 8:20a · Parmesan dairy tour about 8:30 (the earliest start) This is the profile's own example of a "local making-of" experience. Take a fast train to Bologna (37 min), then a local train or taxi to a dairy near Modena. Cheese is made in the early morning, so tours start around 8:30. It's the earliest start of the trip. In the afternoon, Bologna's Pinacoteca holds Raphael's Ecstasy of St Cecilia, back in Room 15 since July 2026. Swap for Lucca + Montecatini Alto (under Options) if you'd rather have an easy start.

Day 6 — Pick up the car. Brolio: castle and vineyard.Thu Jun 10: arrive 10:15a · Castello di Brolio gardens open 10:00 Pick up the car at Florence airport rather than in town; see Watch out for. Drive to Castello di Brolio (about 1¼ h). The gardens, the crypt (the "spooky crypt" from the post) and the ramparts are the castle part. The vineyard tour is the other half: Ricasoli, Chianti's oldest wine family, runs estate tours. Ask for a family-friendly one with grape juice for the kids (check). Lunch at the estate's osteria, then drive 20 minutes to Base 2.

Day 7 — San Gimignano, Monteriggioni, Siena.Fri Jun 11: Leave 8:30a (drive, about an hour) · arrive 9:30a · San Gimignano before the tour buses All three are on one road. In the morning, climb Torre Salvucci Maggiore in San Gimignano, a private tower open to visitors, with views over Tuscany. Skip the queue at the main tower. Wander the old town and stop at Gelateria Dondoli on the main square, which has won world titles. On the way back, walk the walls of Monteriggioni (25 min), a tiny complete walled village. In the late afternoon, go to Siena (20 min more). The Duomo has a marble-inlaid floor and the Piccolomini Library, frescoed by Pinturicchio, the master who worked alongside the young Raphael. Have dinner on the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo as it empties out.

Day 8 — Pienza and pecorino.Sat Jun 12: Leave 9:15a (drive to Pienza, about an hour) · arrive 10:15a · Pecorino dairy tour about 10:30 Take a pecorino dairy tour (about 1½–2 h, starting around 10:30) on a working farm. Lunch at La Terrazza del Chiostro, as requested. Drive back through the Val d'Orcia: the classic cypress-road views, and a stop at Bagno Vignoni, a village built around a steaming thermal pool.

Day 9 — Fly home from FCO (about 2 h) or FLR (about 1½ h). It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

Options (kept from the first draft, for spare days or swaps)

  • Lucca + Montecatini Alto, by train. Rent family bikes on top of Lucca's city walls, a flat 4 km loop through the trees. Then take the 1898 funicular up to Montecatini Alto (8½ minutes, two red cars, Gigio and Gigia). It's a trains-and-walls day with an easy start, and it swaps for Day 5.
  • Fiesole itself. The Roman theatre and the view are a 15-minute walk from the farm. It works for a slow morning.
  • A ninth night. Adding a night near Siena would split Day 7 in two: San Gimignano on one day, Siena and Monteriggioni on the next.
  • Not in the plan: the vineyard horseback ride, because the kids are young and nobody has riding experience. The Leaning Tower of Pisa is also out, because children under 8 can't climb it.

Gelato

Florence is the place to eat all of it. Every shop below is well known; go early in the evening before the queues: - Vivoli (Santa Croce): the oldest in the city. Try the rice gelato. - La Carraia (by Ponte alla Carraia): good-value cones on the way to the Pitti or the Piazzale. - Carabè (near the Accademia): Sicilian gelato and granita. - Gelateria dei Neri (Santa Croce): huge choice, open late. - Perché No! (near the Duomo): one of the oldest, with good dairy-free sorbets. - Dondoli (San Gimignano): the world-champion one on the main square.

Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

Tuscany is better for this than its steak reputation suggests. Order by name: - Beans: fagioli all'uccelletto (white beans in tomato and sage), ribollita (bean-and-bread soup), zuppa di farro e fagioli. - Chickpeas: cecina, a chickpea flatbread sold by the slice around Lucca and Pisa. It's a good snack on the Lucca day. - Cheese and eggs: a pecorino board in Pienza (the trip's best protein day); frittata; uova al pomodoro (eggs in tomato). - Restaurants in Florence: - Il Vegetariano: a Florence institution, with big portions. Ask about the protein dishes; older reviews say they're thin. - Nirvana: roasted seitan. - L'OV Osteria Vegetariana. - Ruth's: vegetarian and kosher, with a fish dish or two each day. It's the easy pick for a mixed table.

Kids' backup: pasta in bianco (with butter and Parmesan) is on every menu even when it isn't printed. Ask for it.

Watch out for

  • ZTL zones. Florence, Siena, San Gimignano and Pienza fine cars that enter their historic centres, and the camera fines arrive months later. Park outside the walls; that's why the car is picked up at the airport.
  • Car seats. Italy requires them for children under 150 cm, which means all four kids. Rental seats are pricey and variable; consider bringing boosters.
  • Museums are closed Mondays. Uffizi, Accademia, Bargello and the Pitti all close that day. Put the day trip (Day 5) or the drive (Day 6) on a Monday as the dates fall.
  • Sunset is late in June (about 8:50 pm). For a 3-year-old, the Piazzale works best with a nap and a pool break first.
