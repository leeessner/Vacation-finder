# Belgium & the Trappist abbeys — draft plan (summer 2027)

**Shape:** 9 nights. This is the Trappist beer trip the profile was waiting
for, and it's built on trains, where **children under 12 ride free**. It has
two castles, the Ghent Altarpiece, Michelangelo's only sculpture to leave
Italy in his lifetime, Rubens in Antwerp, a chocolate workshop, a tram ride
into a cave, and a café with a playground next to the Westvleteren abbey.

## Day by day

**Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight.** Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump **7 hours ahead**, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's **Flights** section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

**Day 1 — Arrive. Train to Bruges.** Walk the canals and the **Markt**, and
have frites on a bench.

**Day 2 — Bruges: belfry, Michelangelo, chocolate.** Climb the **Belfry**
(366 steps, so maybe a parent stays down with the youngest; *check* the age
rules). Then see Michelangelo's **Madonna of Bruges** in the **Church of Our
Lady**, the only sculpture of his to leave Italy in his lifetime. In the
afternoon, take a **chocolate-making workshop**, the local making-of. Finish
with a short **canal boat** ride (30 min).

**Day 3 — Ghent** (25 min by train). **Gravensteen** is a real 12th-century
moated castle with ramparts and a torture museum the 8-year-old will love.
**Under-13s are free.** Then the **Ghent Altarpiece** (van Eyck's *Adoration
of the Mystic Lamb*) in St Bavo's Cathedral, and dinner on the **Graslei**.

**Day 4 — Westvleteren and Ypres (rental car).** Lunch at **In de Vrede**,
the café opposite St Sixtus Abbey. It's the only place you can drink
Westvleteren at the source, and it has an outdoor play area. Then go to
**Ypres** (20 min): the **In Flanders Fields Museum** in the rebuilt Cloth
Hall, and the **Menin Gate**. The Last Post is sounded every evening at 8
pm, which is late but moving.

**Day 5 — Train to Brussels.** See the **Grand Place**, **Manneken Pis** and
a waffle. In the afternoon, the **Belgian Comic Strip Center**: Tintin and
the Smurfs in an Art Nouveau building.

**Day 6 — The Old Masters, then the Atomium.** The **Royal Museums of Fine
Arts** have Bruegel, Rubens, Jordaens and the Flemish primitives. Afterwards,
go up the **Atomium**, the giant steel molecule. You ride an escalator
through the tubes.

**Day 7 — Antwerp** (40 min). The **Cathedral of Our Lady** has two Rubens
altarpieces, *The Raising of the Cross* and *The Descent from the Cross*.
Then **Het Steen**, the riverside castle. **Antwerp Central** station is one
of the world's most beautiful, which earns trains points. The **Rubens
House** is closed for restoration (only the garden is open). Parents' option:
**Café Trappisten** opposite Westmalle Abbey, a bus ride from Antwerp.

**Day 8 — The Han caves.** Take the train to the Ardennes, then a short bus.
At the **Grottes de Han**, a vintage **tram** takes you into the forest to
enormous caves with an underground river. Nearby is **Rochefort**, home of
the Trappist beer; the abbey itself is closed to visitors.

**Day 9 — Brussels, slow.** Choose: **Cantillon**, a working lambic
brewery and museum (a making-of, *check* tours), or parks and shopping.

**Day 10 — Fly home** from BRU. It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

## The Trappist map

Of the world's handful of Trappist breweries, five of Belgium's six can be
reached on this trip:
- **Westvleteren**: In de Vrede (Day 4), with a playground.
- **Westmalle**: Café Trappisten (option on Day 7).
- **Rochefort**: in the town (Day 8); the abbey isn't open.
- **Chimay**: the abbey's inn, the **Auberge de Poteaupré**. It's too far
  for this route; consider it for a car-based variant.
- **Orval**: you can visit the abbey ruins. Same note as Chimay.

Every bar in Belgium pours the rest.

## Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

- **Kaaskroketten** (cheese croquettes), **stoemp** (mash with vegetables,
  often with an egg), Flemish **asparagus with egg** (spring), and a great
  deal of cheese, including Trappist abbey cheeses.
- **Others:** **moules-frites** (mussels and fries) and Flemish beef stew
  cooked in beer.
- **Kids' backup:** frites, waffles, and *spaghetti bolognese*, which is on
  every Belgian café menu.

## Watch out for

- **In de Vrede is closed on Fridays.** Check its calendar before fixing the
  Westvleteren day.
- **Belgian weather:** bring rain layers even in July.
- **Cobblestones:** bring a stroller with real wheels for the 3-year-old.
