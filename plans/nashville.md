**Shape:** 4 nights, by car (4½ h each way). Fri Mar 12 to Tue Mar 16, so

## Day by day

**Day 1 (Fri) — Drive down.** Take I-64/I-57/I-24, with a lunch stop in
**Metropolis, Illinois**, on the Ohio River, for the 15-foot **Superman
statue**. Check in, then walk **Broadway** before dark. Many honky-tonks
admit kids in the daytime and early evening (*check* each door). Have
**hot chicken** for dinner.

**Day 2 (Sat) — The Parthenon, then the oddest cars.** **Centennial Park's
Parthenon** is a full-scale replica, with **Athena**, a 42-foot gilded
statue, inside. It's sculpture on a scale the kids won't forget, and the
park has a playground. In the afternoon, the **Lane Motor Museum** has the
quirkiest car collection in America: amphibious cars, microcars,
one-wheelers, and a kids' play area. It's unique, so it passes the
profile's test. *Evening option:* the **Grand Ole Opry**, the radio show
that has run since 1925. Kids are welcome; it's about 2 hours.

**Day 3 (Sun) — Cheekwood, or the Hermitage.** **Cheekwood** is a mansion
with art and big gardens. In mid-March expect early daffodils and spring
bulbs; the big tulip show, *Cheekwood in Bloom*, is usually in April. It
has two children's areas. *Or* the **Hermitage**, Andrew Jackson's home, for
American history. In the afternoon, a brewery with space for kids (see
below).

**Day 4 (Mon) — The Frist and the farmers' market.** The **Frist Art
Museum** is exhibitions only; *check* what's on in March, since it's worth
it only if it's old masters or sculpture. **Nashville Farmers' Market** for
lunch, and the **Bicentennial Mall** state park next door, with a 200-foot
granite map of Tennessee. In the afternoon, the **Goo Goo Chocolate Co.**
shop downtown (*check* whether its make-your-own-candy class runs that
week). That's the making-of.

**Day 5 (Tue) — Drive home.** *Option:* add **Mammoth Cave** (1½ h north)
for a ranger cave tour in the world's longest cave system. It makes the
drive home about 2 hours longer.

## Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

- **Hot chicken** is the Nashville dish; most places do a hot tofu or
  cauliflower version too (*check*).
- Southern sides are full of beans and cheese: black-eyed peas, mac and
  cheese, pimento cheese.
- **Pancake Pantry** for a family breakfast, if you skip the hotel's.
- **Kids' backup:** burgers, chicken tenders, the Embassy Suites breakfast.

**Beer:** Nashville's craft scene is strong. **Bearded Iris**, **Yazoo** and
**TailGate** have family-friendly taprooms (*check* the age policies).

## Watch out for

- **Spring break overlap.** Many schools share the mid-March week, and
  Nashville is a bachelorette-party magnet on weekends. Broadway after 8 pm
  isn't for kids.
- **Weather:** March can be 40°F or 75°F. Pack for both.
