# Florence & Tuscany — draft plan (summer 2027)

**Shape:** 11 nights. Six on the farm above Florence, five in the countryside
south of Siena with a car. Florence is the art; the countryside is the castles,
towers, cheese and space to run.

Written against `plans/family-profile.md`. Prices were checked in October 2026
from the sources listed at the end. Where those sources disagreed, the higher
figure is used, and anything not confirmed is marked *check*. Euros are
converted at about **$1.15 per €1**.

## Day by day

One main thing in the morning and an easier afternoon, as the profile asks.
The farm's pools and playground are the afternoon plan on most Florence days.

**Day 1 — Arrive.** Settle in at Fattoria di Maiano: pool and the farm.
Dinner at **Cucina Pietraforte**, within walking distance (from the Instagram
post; *check it's open*).

**Day 2 — Pitti Palace & Boboli Gardens.** The **Palatine Gallery** is the
Raphael room of the trip: the *Madonna della Seggiola* and *La Velata* hang
there. Then the Boboli Gardens: grottoes, statues and big lawns, which
the kids can run around. Afternoon at the farm.

**Day 3 — Michelangelo's David, then Leonardo's machines.** Take the first
slot at the **Accademia** (8:15) and allow about an hour. Walk past the Duomo
and the Baptistery's bronze doors. In the afternoon, the **Leonardo
Interactive Museum** has 50+ working models of his inventions to crank and
push. It's the one science museum on the trip, because it's unique.

**Day 4 — Uffizi, early.** Book the 8:15 slot and keep the visit to about 2
hours. Go straight for Raphael's *Madonna of the Goldfinch*, Leonardo's
*Annunciation*, Michelangelo's *Doni Tondo* and Caravaggio's *Medusa*, then
leave while everyone's still happy. Afternoon at the pool.

**Day 5 — Bargello, then a pasta class.** The **Bargello** is the great
sculpture museum: Donatello's *David* and Michelangelo's *Bacchus*. It's small,
so plan about an hour. In the afternoon, a family **pasta class**: tagliatelle
or ravioli, with a "little chef" diploma for the kids at some schools.

**Day 6 — Choose one day trip by train:**
- **(a) Parmesan + Bologna.** This is the profile's own example of a "local
  making-of" experience. Take a fast train to Bologna (37 min), then a local
  train or taxi to a dairy near Modena. Cheese is made in the early morning,
  so tours start around 8:30. It's the earliest start of the trip. In the
  afternoon, Bologna's **Pinacoteca** holds Raphael's *Ecstasy of St
  Cecilia*, back in Room 15 since July 2026.
- **(b) Lucca + Montecatini Alto.** Rent family bikes on top of **Lucca's
  city walls**, a flat 4 km loop through the trees. Then take the 1898
  **funicular** up to **Montecatini Alto** (8½ minutes, two red cars, Gigio and
  Gigia). It's a trains-and-walls day with an easy start.

**Day 7 — Pick up the car, Chianti, Castello di Brolio.** Pick up the car at
Florence airport rather than in town; see *Watch out for*. At **Brolio**,
there are gardens, a crypt (the "spooky crypt" from the post) and lots of
space to run. Lunch at the estate's osteria, then drive to Base 2.

**Day 8 — San Gimignano.** Skip the queue at the main tower and climb **Torre
Salvucci Maggiore** instead. It's a private tower open to visitors, with
views over Tuscany. Wander the old town; this is the profile's "Capri" kind
of morning.

**Day 9 — Siena, and a walled village.** The **Duomo** has a marble-inlaid
floor and the **Piccolomini Library**, frescoed by Pinturicchio, the master
who worked alongside the young Raphael. Then the shell-shaped **Piazza del
Campo**. On the way back, walk the walls of **Monteriggioni**, a tiny
complete walled village. It's castle points for the kids.

**Day 10 — Pienza and pecorino.** A **pecorino dairy tour** (about 1½–2 h,
starting around 10:30) on a working farm. Lunch at **La Terrazza del
Chiostro**, as requested. Drive home through the Val d'Orcia, the classic
cypress-road views.

**Day 11 — Vineyard riding, or a slow day.** **Horseback riding through a
vineyard**, as in the post. Ask about age limits: these rides are often 8+,
and some farms offer ponies for the younger ones. Otherwise, take a pool day.

**Day 12 — Fly home** from FLR or FCO.

## Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

Tuscany is better for this than its steak reputation suggests. Order by name:
- **Beans:** *fagioli all'uccelletto* (white beans in tomato and sage),
  *ribollita* (bean-and-bread soup), *zuppa di farro e fagioli*.
- **Chickpeas:** *cecina*, a chickpea flatbread sold by the slice around
  Lucca and Pisa. It's a good snack on the Lucca day.
- **Cheese and eggs:** a **pecorino** board in Pienza (the trip's best
  protein day); *frittata*; *uova al pomodoro* (eggs in tomato).
- **Restaurants in Florence:**
  - **Il Vegetariano**: a Florence institution, with big portions. Ask about
    the protein dishes; older reviews say they're thin.
  - **Nirvana**: roasted seitan.
  - **L'OV Osteria Vegetariana**.
  - **Ruth's**: vegetarian and kosher, with a fish dish or two each day. It's
    the easy pick for a mixed table.

Kids' backup: pasta *in bianco* (with butter and Parmesan) is on every menu
even when it isn't printed. Ask for it.

## Watch out for

- **ZTL zones.** Florence, Siena, San Gimignano and Pienza fine cars that enter
  their historic centres, and the camera fines arrive months later. Park
  outside the walls; that's why the car is picked up at the airport.
- **Car seats.** Italy requires them for children under 150 cm, which means
  all four kids. Rental seats are pricey and variable; consider bringing
  boosters.
- **Museums are closed Mondays.** Uffizi, Accademia and Bargello all close
  that day. Plan Day 2 (Pitti/Boboli) or the day trip for a Monday as the
  dates fall.
- The **Leaning Tower of Pisa** isn't in the plan: children under 8 can't
  climb it.
