## Day by day

**Day 0 — Travel day: fly overnight.** Leave St. Louis in the afternoon with one connection (usually Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Dallas or an East Coast hub). Fly overnight, and land the next morning, which is Day 1. Allow about 11–15 hours gate to gate. The clocks jump **7 hours ahead**, so Day 1 is for settling in, not sightseeing. The trip page's **Flights** section shows the airports, connection and times of the fare being tracked.

**Day 1 — Arrive.** An evening walk: **Piazza Navona**'s Bernini fountain,
then the **Pantheon**, and gelato at **Giolitti**.

**Day 2 — The Vatican, opened with the Key Master.** Book the **Key
Master's tour** (the "Clavigero" visit): a small group meets the Vatican's
key keeper before dawn, watches the first of the museum's doors being
unlocked, and has the galleries nearly to itself before the crowds arrive.
Be at the entrance by about **6:00** (*check* the exact time, the group
size and whether the 3-year-old can come). It replaces the earliest-slot
ticket. Plan a nap or a slow lunch after it. The **Raphael Rooms** are
the reason to come: *The School of Athens*, *The Fire in the Borgo*, *The
Liberation of St Peter*. Add the **Sistine Chapel** (*check* that the altar
wall restoration has finished), the *Laocoön* and the map gallery. In the
afternoon, see **St Peter's**: Michelangelo's *Pietà* and Bernini's canopy.
Then **climb the dome**; the kids will remember the narrow leaning stair.

**Day 3 — The Colosseum and the Forum.** Arrive at opening, before the heat.
Walk the **Forum** and the **Palatine Hill**, where the emperors lived. Rest
in the afternoon. In the evening, see the **Trevi Fountain** after dark and
the **Spanish Steps**.

**Day 4 — Galleria Borghese.** It's the profile's perfect museum: small and
first-rate. Bernini's *Apollo and Daphne* and *Rape of Proserpina* (the hand
in the marble thigh), Caravaggio's *Boy with a Basket of Fruit*, and
Raphael's *Deposition*. Visits are timed 2-hour slots. Afterwards, rent a
**family bike** or a **surrey** in the Villa Borghese park, and catch the
sunset at the **Pincio** terrace.

**Day 5 — Castel Sant'Angelo, then Caravaggio's chapels.** Hadrian's tomb
became the popes' **fortress**. It has ramparts, cannonballs, a secret
escape corridor to the Vatican, and the angel on top. In the afternoon,
see three Caravaggios for free in **San Luigi dei Francesi** (the St Matthew
cycle), then the **Galleria Doria Pamphilj**, with Velázquez's *Innocent X*
and more Caravaggio. Have dinner in **Trastevere**.

**Day 6 — Ostia Antica.** Take the train 30 min to Rome's ancient port: a
whole Roman town of streets, shops, a theatre and latrines, with room to
roam and few crowds. It's a better kids' ruin than the Forum. Go to the
beach at Ostia after, if everyone still has energy.

**Day 7 — Galleria Colonna or a pasta class.** The **Galleria Colonna**, a
favourite from the profile, opens only on certain days, usually Friday and
Saturday mornings (*check*). Otherwise, take a **family pasta class**, or the
**gladiator school** near the Appian Way, where the kids train with wooden
swords (*check* ages).

**Day 8 — Fly home** from FCO. It's a full travel day home: westbound flights usually leave late morning or midday and land in St. Louis that evening, about 12–16 hours gate to gate. You gain back the 7 hours.

## Gelato

The well-known names: **Giolitti** (near the Pantheon, since 1900),
**Fatamorgana** (unusual flavours, several branches), **Gelateria del
Teatro** (near Navona), and **Otaleg** in Trastevere.

## Food — and protein for the vegetarian parent

- Roman classics with legumes: **pasta e ceci** (chickpeas) and **pasta e
  fagioli** (beans).
- Cheese dishes: **cacio e pepe**, **supplì** (fried rice balls with
  mozzarella), and Roman-Jewish **carciofi** (artichokes) in the Ghetto.
- Vegetarian restaurants: **Il Margutta**, vegetarian since 1979.
- **Pizza al taglio** by weight at **Bonci's Pizzarium** near the Vatican.
- **Kids' backup:** pasta in bianco, pizza, supplì.

## Watch out for

- **Heat:** Rome in July is 33°C+. Do the outdoor ruins at opening.
- **Dress code** at St Peter's and the Vatican: covered shoulders and knees
  for everyone.
- **Fake "skip the line" sellers** outside the Vatican. Buy only on the
  official sites.
